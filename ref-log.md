Implementing the Planner–Reviewer multi-agent workflow taught me how to separate knowledge roles and leverage both internal reasoning and real world checks. The Planner’s responsibility is creative, constraint aware itinerary design without internet reliance. This lets it propose flexible and human friendly plans quickly. The Reviewer’s role is complementary: validating real world feasibility by checking opening hours, booking requirements, and realistic travel times. This separation improved clarity and reduced risky assumptions.

Key challenges included designing instruction prompts that produce consistent, machine understandable formats and ensuring the Reviewer’s web checks are focused and limited (so the tool isn’t overused). Initially the Planner produced verbose prose that was hard for the Reviewer to inspect programmatically. I resolved this by enforcing a strict “Day X” format and short bulletized activity lines. Another challenge was selecting which items the Reviewer should verify in real time. I chose a conservative heuristic of "top 5 load-bearing items" to balance coverage and speed.

Design choices I found useful: a “Delta List” output from the Reviewer that prioritizes changes (Critical/Recommended/Optional) and a short “Assumptions” block in the Planner output, so the Reviewer knows what to verify first. I also experimented with reviewer personas (e.g., “Logistics Reviewer” vs “Cost Reviewer”) during development, but found a single general Reviewer with targeted checks was simpler to integrate.

Tools used: the provided internet_search helper (for live validation); no other external generative assistance was used beyond iterative prompt tuning.
